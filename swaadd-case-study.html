01 — Overview

The Challenge

Nepal's food delivery market is growing fast, but the existing apps — Foodmandu, Bhojdeals and others — share a visual identity that feels imported rather than built for Nepal. Generic flat icons, neutral colour palettes, and interfaces that could belong to any city in South Asia. Swaadd (स्वाद) — meaning "taste" in Nepali — is a concept for a food delivery app designed from the ground up for Kathmandu, with a visual language that reflects local culture.

The goal was a complete hi-fidelity design handoff: 4 separate apps (Customer, Seller, Rider, Admin), a unified design system, and full Figma component specs — everything a development team would need to build.

🎨

Generic Visual Identity

Existing apps look identical to international competitors — no local cultural identity or visual language rooted in Nepal.

📱

No Nepal-First Design System

No food delivery app in Nepal had a documented design system with Devanagari type, local colour tokens, and NPR-first pricing.

🔄

Fragmented User Roles

Customer, seller and rider experiences are often crammed into one app — creating cognitive overload for each user type.

💳

Payment Integration Gaps

Nepali users rely on eSewa, Khalti and COD. Most apps treat these as afterthoughts rather than primary payment flows.

04 — Design Principles

What Shaped Every Decision

🇳🇵
Nepal-First Identity
Every colour, typeface and icon choice should feel rooted in Nepali culture — not borrowed from a generic template.
🔀
One System, Four Apps
All 4 apps share one design system. Users switching between roles should feel instant familiarity.
⚡
Speed Over Decoration
Delivery apps are used under time pressure. Every screen should resolve the user's need in the fewest possible taps.
💳
Local Payments First
eSewa and Khalti are not secondary options — they are the primary payment methods for Nepali users.
📐
Handoff-Ready Components
Every component is documented with tokens, spacing values, and state variants — developer-ready from day one.
🌐
Bilingual by Design
Devanagari and Latin text must coexist naturally. Mukta was chosen as the typeface because it was built for exactly this.

07 — Key Design Decisions

Why I Designed It This Way

01
Deep Red as Primary
Red (#B5281C) was chosen as primary not just for appetite appeal, but because it echoes Nepali cultural associations — temples, festivals, sindoor. It makes Swaadd feel distinctly local.
02
Mukta as the Typeface
Mukta handles both Devanagari and Latin at the same weight and optical size — the only typeface that lets "Swaadd" and "स्वाद" sit on the same line without visual tension.
03
15-Second Rider Timer
The accept/decline timer was set to 15 seconds after benchmarking against Pathao and Foodmandu — long enough to read the order details, short enough to keep delivery SLAs realistic.
04
eSewa Login on Welcome Screen
Rather than a standard email/password login, the customer app leads with eSewa login — meeting Nepali users at the payment method they already trust and use daily.
05
Role-Specific App Colors
Customer uses Primary Red, Seller uses Green, Rider uses Blue, Admin uses Saffron — all from the same palette but unmistakably different. Users always know which role they're in.
06
Design Tokens Over Hardcoded Values
Every colour, spacing and type size is a named token — not a hex code typed inline. This makes the handoff reliable and ensures the developer builds what was designed.
08 — Reflections

What I Learned

  • 01
    Designing for multiple user roles requires systems thinking — when 4 apps share one design system, every component decision has 4x the consequences. Tokens aren't optional — they're what keeps everything consistent.
  • 02
    Cultural identity is a design decision — choosing Mukta over Inter, and deep red over orange, wasn't aesthetic preference. It was a deliberate choice to make Swaadd feel like it belongs in Kathmandu.
  • 03
    Hi-fi without wireframes first is dangerous — I built this as a hi-fi first pass, and some screen flows were revised 3-4 times because structural problems only appeared at pixel level. Wireframes (like the खाना App document) should always come first.
  • 04
    Payment UX is product UX — in Nepal, how you pay is as important as what you're buying. Treating eSewa and Khalti as primary — not secondary — changed the entire onboarding flow design.
